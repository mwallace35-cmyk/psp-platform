## A Brian Daly by Any Other Name

The first Catholic League championship Joe Colistra ever won came on a cold December afternoon at Villanova Stadium in 1989. His La Salle Explorers beat Roman Catholic 13-0 to claim the Hawks' first Catholic League football title since 1960. The hero of the day was a 5-11, 185-pound senior defensive back named Brian Daly — no relation to the other Brian Daly, the 1988 Monsignor Bonner basketball star who had set a game-record 30 points in that year's CL basketball title game. Same name, spelled the same way. Different generation. Both championship-makers.

{{PhotoInline id="colistra-portrait"}}

The La Salle Brian Daly had come into the game without an interception all season long. In the first half, he picked off Roman QB Jim McGeehan once. In the second, he did it twice more — the first defensive back in Philadelphia championship-game history to grab three interceptions in the title game. Then, just for effect, he took the second-half kickoff back 72 yards for a touchdown.

{{PullQuote id="daly-name"}}

{{PullQuote id="first-interceptions"}}

Along with Jerry Rock and Jim McGoldrick (one interception each), La Salle's secondary limited McGeehan — who had been fighting the flu all week — to 6-for-27 passing for 84 yards. The Explorers' own offense managed only 51 yards and two first downs (one of those on a penalty). It was still enough. La Salle 13, Roman 0. Colistra had his first championship.

## The Streak — 1995 to 1997

Six years later, Colistra had Brett Gordon at quarterback. He also had a defense, an offensive line, and one of the best two-year stretches any coach in Philadelphia high school football had ever put together.

Game 3 of the 1995 season began what would become a city-record 32-game winning streak. The Explorers ran off 11 straight to end 1995, including a 14-7 Catholic League championship win over Archbishop Ryan at Northeast High. Running back Mike Durso, wearing heavy padding to protect a fractured left thumb, ran 10 times for 43 yards and a touchdown. Kyle Mallach added 50 yards and a score. Brett Gordon, a dual-threat QB finishing his junior year, went 9-for-18 for 109 yards — ending the season with 2,136 yards and 23 total touchdowns.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1995"}}

Then came 1996, the single most historic season in La Salle football history. The Explorers ran the table: 14-0, the first 14-win season in Philadelphia city-leagues football history. They extended their winning streak to 25 games. And they did it without a single 100-yard rushing performance by any of their backs — until Game 14, the Catholic League championship against Father Judge at Northeast High.

{{PhotoInline id="colistra-sideline"}}

In that one, on a field made sloppy-to-treacherous by overnight rain, Mike Durso carried 22 times for 113 yards and a touchdown. Brett Gordon went 16-for-33 for 162 yards and a TD to Jeff Pietrak, plus field goals of 23 and 34 yards from Gordon's backup role as a kicker. La Salle won 20-7.

Gordon finished his two-year varsity career with 4,190 passing yards and 51 touchdowns — city-record numbers at the time. The 32-game winning streak ended midway through the 1997 season, but the mark — 32 consecutive Catholic League football wins — still stands in the city record book.

## 1998 — Gabe Marabella's Moment

Colistra's fourth and final Catholic League championship came in 1998. In the CL final at Northeast High against Archbishop Ryan, starting quarterback Chris Pennington was moved to running back midway through the game — a bold in-game adjustment from Colistra that opened up running lanes. Pennington finished with 17 carries for 135 yards and two touchdowns (including a 47-yard clincher with 1:45 left). Sophomore backup QB Gabe Marabella, playing the biggest moment of his young football life, came off the bench and went 6-for-9 for 117 yards to help close the game. Senior kicker Mike Savage booted a 39-yard field goal — his 11th of the season, tying the Catholic League single-season record. Chris Dougherty led the defense with 10 tackles.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1998"}}

Colistra's record in championship games: 4-0. His 1998 title brought the Explorers to four CL championships in 10 seasons.

## The 21-Year Ledger

Final totals: 96-52-1 in the Catholic League, 153-88-2 overall. Four CL championships. The city-record 32-game winning streak. The first 14-0 season in Philadelphia city-leagues history. And a quarterback in Brett Gordon whose 4,190 passing yards and 51 career touchdowns still sit near the top of the Catholic League record book a quarter-century later.
