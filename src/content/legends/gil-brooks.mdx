## Ending a Twenty-Year Drought

Before Gil Brooks arrived at St. Joseph's Prep in 1992, the Hawks had not won a Catholic League championship since 1977. Five seasons into his tenure, in 1997, one persistent student took matters into his own hands. Early in the week of the championship game, handwritten signs began appearing on walls all over the Prep's Girard Avenue campus: "Don't concentrate on schoolwork. Concentrate on winning the championship." Administrators kept ripping them down. The signs kept coming back.

{{PhotoInline id="brooks-portrait"}}

{{PullQuote id="rallying-cry"}}

Saturday night at Northeast High, in front of 8,000 shivering fans, the Hawks stunned Archbishop Ryan 14-3 for their first Catholic League title since 1977 — a 20-year drought broken. Hundreds of SJP students stormed the field from the visiting sideline. One of them wore nothing but shorts. Another wore only a diaper. Forty minutes after the final whistle, Brooks was still on the field, still taking hugs from well-wishers.

The game itself belonged to sophomore Milton Johnson, who ran 20 times for 93 yards to finish the season with 1,032 — making him and Ryan's Joe O'Connell (1,147) the first two sophomores in Catholic League history to rush for 1,000 yards. Aaron Brown broke a 62-yard touchdown run. Jermaine Slade caught a 6-yard scoring pass from Steve Comly. Kevin Komelasky led the defense with nine tackles.

## The Pat Kaiser Year — 2002

In 2001, Brooks's Hawks opened the season 0-3 in non-league play. They then ran off ten straight wins and beat Bonner 31-6 for the Catholic Red crown at Northeast High behind a Vince Gallagher 71-yard touchdown bomb to Pete Chromiak. Pat Kaiser ran four yards for a touchdown to make it 21-0 at halftime. The comeback season produced a 10-3 finish.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-2001"}}

A year later, the program gave way to Pat Kaiser. In the 2002 Catholic Red title game against Roman Catholic — held before Thanksgiving for the first time in decades — Matt Stefanski threw a 59-yard touchdown pass to Steve Quinn on SJP's first offensive play. Then Kaiser took over. In that one game, he set the city postseason records for rushing yards (316 on 18 carries) and points (32), tied the city marks for rushing touchdowns in a playoff game (4) and career field goals (25), and helped the Hawks walk off with a 38-7 win.

Kaiser finished the 2002 season with 2,178 rushing yards, 35 rushing touchdowns, and 37 total touchdowns — all city records at the time. His 525 career points rank No. 1 in Catholic League history. His 306 season points briefly held the Pennsylvania state record.

## The Streak — 35 Straight

The 2002 championship started something bigger. Brooks's 2003 Hawks rolled into the season with a 13-0 record the year before, and by midseason 2003 they were chasing — and then breaking — the city record for consecutive wins. La Salle's 1995-97 run of 33 straight had been the mark. The Hawks blew past it on a Saturday night at Northeast High, jolting Father Judge 42-0 in a Catholic Red game to push the streak to 33.

{{PhotoInline id="brooks-championship"}}

Senior guard Mike "Bus" Buscaglia — a 5-10, 245-pound, 380-pound-bench-press, 785-pound-squat, never-miss-an-offseason-workout self-made contributor — led the line that carried the offense. Brooks joked afterward about naming the Prep's weight room after him. Time-sharing tailbacks Dan Jones (8 carries, 130 yards, three TDs of 26, 78, and 10 yards) and sophomore John Shaw (3 carries, 141 yards, TDs of 70 and 68 yards) averaged a first down every ten plays.

Two weeks later, Brooks's Hawks extended the streak to 35 games in the Catholic Red title game against Cardinal O'Hara at Plymouth-Whitemarsh High. Steve Quinn recovered the opening kickoff on the O'Hara 11. Dan Jones ran for a touchdown on the next snap. Sophomore John Shaw then tied a city postseason record with four rushing touchdowns on 19 carries for 98 yards. The Hawks won 45-7, finished 12-0, and — in Brooks's own phrasing — represented the best team in school history.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-2003"}}

## The Fifth Title — Tying Dick Bedesem

Two years later, Brooks came looking for his fifth Catholic League championship. A fifth would tie him with the late Dick Bedesem of Bishop Egan for the most CL titles by any coach in league history. The Hawks got it against Cardinal O'Hara at Northeast High in 2005 — a 41-7 rout, the team's fourth Red title in five years.

Sophomore Jamir Livingston ran 32 times for 187 yards and four touchdowns (tying the city postseason record). Classmate Tim Edger broke the city postseason kicking-points record with 11 (two field goals, five PATs). The Hawks outgained O'Hara 352-26 and held the Lions to one first down.

The game also produced one of Brooks's favorite Gil Brooks stories. Senior defensive lineman Charlie Noonan had been sidelined in mid-September after an offensive tackle fell against his left leg in practice (strained MCL, a month off). He hadn't carried the ball since the opener. Midway through the fourth quarter, starting fullback Ryan Bradley needed the sideline to tend to a busted lip. Noonan got the wave.

On his first carry, he rumbled 25 yards to the O'Hara 3, nearly burying the defender who brought him down. Two plays later, he ran in from two yards out. Afterward — laughing — Noonan explained: "I've been wanting to run all year. I never bugged anyone because Ryan was doing nice things for us. But I was a fullback in grade school [Sacred Heart, Manoa] and scored a lot of TDs. To get this one last chance was nice."

Noonan also led the defense that night with 11 stops. The Noonan family — Tom (now at Pitt, starting guard for the 2002 title team) and Mark (Wesleyan, QB of the 2003 champions) — had now produced three brothers who walked off with a championship.

## The 18-Year Ledger

Brooks's final tally at St. Joseph's Prep: 162-57-2, 101-19-2 in Catholic League play, five CL championships, one city-record winning streak, and a program-long list of Inter-Ac and FBS college football alumni that kept stretching every year he coached. He finished tied with Dick Bedesem for the most championships in Catholic League football history.
