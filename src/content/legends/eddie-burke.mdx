## Two Schools, Two Championships, One Year Apart

In March 1971, Eddie Burke's St. Joseph's Prep beat Cardinal O'Hara 64-58 at the Palestra to win the Catholic League championship. Senior guard Maurice "Mo" Howard went 12-for-18 from the field and 5-for-8 from the free-throw line for 29 points — a CL postseason record. Bill Truskey added 13 points and 12 rebounds. The Hawklets had taken a 49-46 lead in the fourth on two Howard baskets and a Truskey hoop, and never gave it back.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1971"}}

The next year, Burke was coaching a different team at a different school. He had moved to St. Thomas More — a once-powerful South Philadelphia Catholic League program — and brought a group of players with him into the 1971-72 season. In March 1972, St. Thomas More beat North Catholic 54-42 at the Palestra to win the Catholic League championship. Carl Kenty collected 13 points, 19 rebounds, and eight blocks. Emery Sammons scored 16. Larry Sanders grabbed 13 boards. North's fans, apparently not pleased with the result, pelted the court with stink bombs and then — as the Golden Bears celebrated — with empty beer cans.

Eddie Burke had just become the first coach in Catholic League history to win back-to-back championships at different schools.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1972"}}

A week later, in the same building, St. Thomas More added the 1972 City Title with a 53-45 win over Bartram. Ron Holmes scored 15. Emery Sammons added 12. Carl Kenty had 11. Larry Sanders pulled down 15 rebounds. The Golden Bears stormed out to a 17-2 lead in the first 6:59 and never looked back. A future NBA player — Public League Bartram star Joe "Jelly Bean" Bryant — scored 18 points in the losing effort, miles from the father-son story (his son Kobe would play his own high school basketball at Lower Merion two decades later).

## The Catholic League Years

After the St. Thomas More dynasty, Burke spent three more years with the Golden Bears, then single seasons at Bishop McDevitt (1976) and West Catholic (1977) before leaving the Catholic League altogether. He returned for a second St. Joseph's Prep stint from 1993 through 1999 — a harder stretch, with the Hawks going 3-22 in his first year back and gradually rebuilding to 14-11 in his fourth. In total, Burke logged 16 Catholic League seasons across four schools.

## Drexel and the 1986 NCAA Berth

In 1978, Burke left the Catholic League and took over Drexel's Division I program. He spent the next 14 seasons rebuilding the Dragons into an East Coast Conference contender. The culmination came on March 8, 1986, at Towson Center in Maryland, where Drexel beat Hofstra 80-76 in the ECC Championship game behind junior point guard Michael Anderson's 21 points, 8 rebounds, 3 assists, and 5 steals — unanimous MVP honors. Senior co-captain Walt Fuller added 17. John Rankin contributed 16 points and 7 rebounds.

It was Drexel's eighth consecutive win, its 14th in 15 games, and the program's first NCAA Tournament berth in history. The Dragons' 19 wins that season tied a Division I school record previously set in 1982, when Burke had taken Drexel to its first ECC championship game (a loss to St. Joseph's).

Drexel got a high seed and a first-round matchup in the NCAA Tournament. The game ended Drexel's season but not its place in history: Burke had taken a "pretty good basketball team" (his phrase) and put it on every sports page in America.

## NBA Alumni

Anderson, the point guard from Drexel's 1986 championship, became a two-time East Coast Conference Player of the Year (1986, 1988) under Burke and went on to a long NBA career. Along with Maurice "Mo" Howard from the 1971 St. Joseph's Prep team, Anderson was one of two NBA players Burke developed across his 30 years on the bench.

## The Coaching Tree

Burke's 1971 St. Joseph's Prep championship team also produced one of the most accomplished coaches in 21st-century college basketball: Phil Martelli, a sophomore guard on that team, later coached St. Joseph's University to the 2004 NCAA Elite Eight and an undefeated regular season. Martelli's name sits in the front row of the 1971 SJP title photo, and Burke is standing in the back row with his hand on a manager's shoulder.
