## The Recruiter Who Became a Track Dynasty

Fred Rosenfeld did not grow up a track coach. He played soccer in high school. When Overbrook hired him in the late 1960s to teach physical education to the afternoon shift — the school was so overcrowded that kids attended in half-day shifts — he was asked to help supervise the morning workout sessions of the track team. He tried it. He loved it. Within a decade, he had turned Overbrook into the dominant boys track program in the Public League, and eventually — after moving to Central in 1994 — he repeated the feat at a second school.

{{PhotoInline id="rosenfeld-portrait"}}

By the time he retired in June 2004, Rosenfeld had won 17 solo Public League boys outdoor track championships, 16 Public League boys cross country titles, and five Public League girls cross country titles — in just six years of coaching the girls' side. He had been inducted into the Pennsylvania State Track & Field Hall of Fame. He was one of only 10 coaching inductees at the time. He and Tim Hickey (William Penn) were the only two Philadelphia-area coaches in the shrine.

{{PullQuote id="hallway-recruit"}}

Current and former Rosenfeld athletes tell the same story: he walked the Overbrook and Central hallways every morning, looking for kids who looked remotely athletic, and asked them all the same question. Many of them had never run a step in an organized meet. They left four years later with Public League medals, scholarship offers, and — in the case of Jason Grimes — the world's No. 2 long jump ranking.

## Nine Straight at Overbrook — 1977 through 1985

The foundational accomplishment of Rosenfeld's career was the nine-in-a-row from 1977 through 1985 at Overbrook. No Philadelphia high school track program had ever put together a streak like it. Over those nine seasons his Panthers racked up scoring margins like 145.5 in 1993, 176 in 1983, 171.5 in 1984, 145 in 1979 — always scoring 110-plus points and always finishing first in every relay and field event that mattered.

{{PhotoInline id="rosenfeld-overbrook"}}

The ninth title, in 1985, was the most personal. Rosenfeld had been told by a critic before the season that "you better go read your press clippings because you're never going to do anything again." He had rumbled about it all spring. When his team held off Central 154-127 to win the ninth straight, the normally unemotional coach finally let the bitterness show.

{{PullQuote id="press-clippings"}}

The meet belonged to senior David Moore, a comeback story who had missed most of his junior year after a car accident. He won the 800 meters in 1:53.9 — a meet record — and the 1,600 meters in 4:25.5.

{{PullQuote id="moore-comeback"}}

Assistant coach Mervin Jones laughed afterward: "This was the first time I ever had to pick Fred up. He was saying in the wintertime, 'Forget it. We have no chance.' I said to him, 'We're still breathing, aren't we?'"

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-1985"}}

## The Jason Grimes Year

A year before Moore's comeback, Rosenfeld had coached a 1977 Overbrook graduate named Jason Grimes — by then the world's No. 2 long jumper of 1984 and the holder of a Public League long jump record (23 feet, 8½ inches) that would stand for 18 years. Grimes' name hangs over the entire Overbrook era like a banner. When Rosenfeld wanted his 2004 Central stars Mike Syrnick and Mike Wray to understand what greatness looked like, he arranged a dinner for them with Grimes during the indoor nationals in Landover, Maryland.

## The Move to Central — Same Script, New Cast

After 17 seasons at Overbrook, Rosenfeld requested a transfer. The move to Central was complicated (Steve Korsin still held the head job), so Rosenfeld co-coached alongside Arnie Shiffrin in 1995 and 1996, then took over outright in 1997. His first solo title at Central came in 1998, behind a 178-point meet that is still remembered for senior Napoleon Nelson — a 5-7, 165-pound worker bee who had offered his spot on the 4×100 relay to a faster teammate two years earlier and was now back running the leadoff leg for a title-winning relay.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-1998"}}

The wins kept coming. 1998, 1999 (203 points), 2000 (184 points), 2002 (170.5), 2003 (216), 2004 (214.5). In the 2004 finale, Rosenfeld's Central team more than doubled runner-up Murrell Dobbins Tech (100 points). Mike Syrnick swept the 800 and 1,600. Mike Wray took the 100m high hurdles. Patrick Carr won the 3,200. Mike Taras — an honor-roll student with asthma who had been with Rosenfeld since cross country — contributed third-place finishes in the 1,600 and 3,200. Depth wins championships. It always had.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-2004"}}

## What He Got Out of It

Rosenfeld retired in June 2004 and took with him 17 solo PL boys track championships, 21 cross country championships, a Hall of Fame plaque, and a 36-year marriage to his wife Wendy. When Ted Silary asked him what he'd gotten out of nearly three decades of pre-dawn workouts, pre-meet motivation, and post-meet scorekeeping, the coach who had built the Overbrook dynasty and then built another at Central gave the same answer he'd always given.
