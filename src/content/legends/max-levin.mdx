## The Last Great Old-School City Title

Mark "Max" Levin's defining moment as Overbrook basketball coach came at the Palestra in the spring of 1980. The Panthers were 33-0. The last great old-school City Title — the annual game between the Public League champion and the Catholic League champion — was set for them against Roman Catholic. Roman had 6-5 senior forward Lonnie McFarlan, the Markward Award winner and a McDonald's All-American. Overbrook had Tony Costner, a 6-10 center who had been named to Parade Magazine's fourth-team All-American list earlier that same week.

Regulation ended 50-50. Levin pulled his players into the huddle. "I just said three little words," he told reporters afterward: "'Get the ball to [Costner].'" In the overtime period that followed, Costner scored eight of his 23 points, making free throws, drawing fouls, and turning two Darryl Brown feeds into layups. By the end of the extra period, Overbrook had won 65-56 and Levin had his second consecutive City Title in what would be the last old-school title series ever played.

## Back-to-Back Perfect — 1979 and 1980

The 1980 team's 34-0 capped a two-year stretch during which Overbrook went 68-1 — one of the best two-year runs any Public League basketball team has ever put together. The 1979 champions featured Ricky Tucker, Joe Washington, Jeffrey Tucker, Richard Congo, and a then-junior Tony Costner. Costner's senior year brought Steve Black, Darryl "World" Brown, John Bryant, and William "Vernon" Hardwick into the starting lineup. Nothing the Public League threw at them mattered. The Catholic League champion barely survived them in the City Title game. Nothing in Philadelphia high school basketball that year was better than the 1980 Panthers.

## Costner and Costner's Pipeline

Before Tony Costner, Levin's Panthers had produced Lewis Lloyd — a 21.6-point-a-game scorer in 1977 who later played in the NBA. After Costner came Ahmad Gilbert and Herman "Coozie" Willis, who led the 1983 championship team. And at the very end of Levin's run, in 1989, a guard named Mike Tyson (not the boxer, no relation) led the Public League in scoring at 24.4 points per game as a senior.

Across his 18 seasons, Levin's program produced 15 of Overbrook's top career scorers — a list that includes Lewis Lloyd, Tony Costner, Michael "Moon" Black, Mike Blackshear (twice), Carlton "C-9" Willis, Rich Laurel, Jack Congo, Ahmad Gilbert, Ricky Tucker, Joe Washington, Timmy Pounds, Herman Willis, Ed Urie, and Mike Tyson.
