## The Streak

From the opening kickoff of the 1990 season until a quarterfinal game on a Saturday night in the fall of 1994, Archbishop Ryan did not lose a Catholic League football game. Forty-seven consecutive games. Forty-five wins. Two ties. Twenty-six shutouts. Four straight championships. Five Northern Division regular-season titles. An average of 3.3 points allowed per game.

{{PhotoInline id="galeone-portrait"}}

Coach Glen Galeone and defensive coordinator Lee Marvel built the Raiders into a program that hit harder, ran longer after practice, and trusted the preparation more than any Catholic League team of its era. They were almost always smaller than the opponent. It almost never mattered.

{{PhotoInline id="streak-graphic"}}

## 1990 — The Overtime Opener

The streak started on ordinary ground and ended in extraordinary fashion. In the 1990 Catholic League title game at Villanova Stadium, Ryan and Archbishop Carroll played the first overtime final in CL history. Jamie Sutton's 15-yard touchdown pass to Jim Stott decided it, 20-13. Brian Hamill kicked field goals of 32 and 39 yards that afternoon and finished the season with a city-league single-season record 11 field goals in 16 attempts. Fullback Mark Ostaszewski ran 35 times for 130 yards. Carroll's Sean McGarvey returned a kickoff 86 yards for a score.

## 1991 — "Ryan Doesn't Have It Anymore"

A year later, the Raiders took a Thanksgiving loss to archrival George Washington — their first ever to Washington on Turkey Day, a 28-24 collapse after leading 21-0 at halftime. A suburban sports editor, covering neither team, predicted that St. James would roll Ryan 33-13 in the championship game. Someone at Ryan's practice field painted "St. James #1" on a goal post. Galeone needed no further motivational material.

{{PullQuote id="respect"}}

Ryan won 13-0 in front of 5,000 at La Salle University. Mike Erbrick ran 31 times for 147 yards. Brian Hamill kicked field goals of 28 and 34 yards. The Raiders became the first Catholic League team in 29 years to go through the playoffs without allowing a point. Erbrick finished the season with 1,774 rushing yards (No. 2 in CL history) and 14 touchdowns. The defense had now yielded just 29 points in 10 games against Catholic League opposition — the stingiest full-season effort in the CL since Cardinal O'Hara's 23 points allowed in 1979. In 20 league games across 1990 and 1991, Ryan allowed only a single rushing touchdown.

## 1992 — First Unbeaten Season in 25 Years

The 1992 championship game at La Salle University was the streak at its highest expression. Against Roman Catholic, Ryan's defense — led by Gene McAleer's six tackles and Dave McDonald's three interceptions (one returned 34 yards for a touchdown) — held the Cahillites to 60 yards of total offense in a 25-8 win. Jaime Brown added 24 carries for 72 yards and a score. The 12-0-1 season was the first unbeaten campaign in Ryan's 25 years of Catholic League football.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1992"}}

## 1993 — The Fourth in a Row

The fourth championship arrived in a 10-0 defensive grind against Archbishop Carroll at La Salle University. Bill McVey ran five yards for a touchdown on a tight-end reverse. Kevin Wyszynski kicked a 36-yard field goal. That was all the scoring. With the win, Ryan became the first CL team ever to finish unbeaten against league opposition for four consecutive years, going 38-0-2 across that stretch. Marvel's defense had held CL opponents to 3.6 points per game over that four-year span with 21 shutouts.

> "Gene is totally out for the team. That's why we respect him so much." — Paul Nice, Ryan inside linebacker, on McAleer

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1993"}}

## 1994 — The End

The streak finally ended on a Saturday night at Northeast High School, where Ryan came into a playoff quarterfinal against Bishop McDevitt — the same team that had handed the Raiders their last Catholic League loss five years earlier, a 16-12 decision in a 1989 quarterfinal. McDevitt's Chris Westerman blocked a Kevin Wyszynski punt midway through the first quarter. Pat Watkins recovered the ball in the Ryan end zone. Ryan, limited to 101 yards of total offense, never answered. McDevitt 10, Ryan 0. The streak was over at 47 games, 45 wins, two ties.

{{PullQuote id="mcfillin-stunned"}}

Center Matt McFillin — who had been a ball boy for the first two years of the streak, a little-used sub for the third, a contributor for the fourth, and a starting captain for the fifth — stood in the postgame locker room stunned. "I'm not sure anybody realizes what we did," he said. "When you — five years and everybody's dying to beat you…"

Six shutouts in 10 league games in 1990. 26 shutouts over 47 games. 3.3 points allowed per game. Four championships. Five Northern Division regular-season titles. First Raiders team to go 12-0-1 in the Catholic League. First CL team in 29 years through the playoffs unscored upon. First CL team to go four consecutive years unbeaten in league play. An unbeaten run that ran nearly five full calendar years.
