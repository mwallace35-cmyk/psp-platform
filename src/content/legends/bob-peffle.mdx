## Nineteen Seasons at Oxford and Wakeling

Bob Peffle's Frankford High baseball classroom sat at Oxford Avenue and Wakeling Street, a few blocks from the stadium where Al Angelo had won 10 Public League football titles. Peffle — himself a Frankford class of 1965 infielder who went on to Temple and spent a few summers in the Houston Astros farm system — took over the baseball program in 1989. For the next 19 years, he built a consistent winner that produced five Public League championships, 15 Daily News All-City honorees, and one umpire's tearful autograph request.

{{PhotoInline id="peffle-portrait"}}

## 2000 — Ending the 19-Year Drought

Peffle's first championship arrived in 2000, ending Frankford's 19-year baseball title drought dating to 1981. Senior lefthander Ed Durfor allowed seven hits, reached base three times (two walks plus a hit-by-pitch), and stole three bases as the Pioneers won 6-0. Jim Connolly drove in two on a groundout and a double; Russ Pizzo and Mike Tritz added RBI singles. Durfor, named that year's Daily News Player of the Year, would later earn a significant place in Peffle program lore for reasons that had nothing to do with baseball.

## The Joe Farina Era — 2003 and 2004

The defining stretch of Peffle's coaching career was the Joe Farina years. Farina, a 6-1, 220-pound righthander, arrived at Frankford after transferring from Northeast (where his brother R.J. had been a PL champion starter), and spent the next three years collecting championships across three sports. By the time he graduated, he had won seven Public League titles: two in baseball, three in wrestling, and two in football. Only his sophomore baseball season — a 2002 semifinal loss to Girard Academic Music Program — left an empty finger on his championship hand. In overall PL play across all three sports, he finished 97-2.

The first baseball title came in 2003. Frankford ran the table, finishing 17-0 in the Public League (one league game canceled) and 19-0 overall. In the championship game against Northeast at La Salle University, Farina threw a four-hitter with six strikeouts. Matt Colon doubled and scored in a two-run third. Adam Hartman doubled in a run. Cory Shaeffer tripled in an insurance run in the sixth. Final: 3-1.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-2003"}}

The return trip to the championship game the next year added another layer. Northeast fans — still angry about Farina's transfer — bellowed "Traitor!" from the first-base dugout stands for most of the game. Farina, naturally, thought it was hilarious ("The way they wanted it. The way I wanted it. We came out on top"). Pitching a seven-hitter with 10 strikeouts, he collected two hits of his own and drove in a run on a bases-loaded walk. Cory Shaeffer and Richard Jimenez had RBI sacrifice flies. Jimenez added an RBI single. Final: Frankford 6, Northeast 4 — Farina's seventh title in three years.

After the championship, assistant coach Juan Namnun (Frankford's coach from 2008 on) told reporters in a voice thick with emotion that Ed Durfor — the 2000 Player of the Year who was now a soldier in Baghdad — had twice called the Frankford program that season to wish the kids luck.

## The 2005 Eight-Run First

Frankford's fourth title of the Peffle era came in 2005. Against Central at La Salle University, the Pioneers erupted for an eight-run first inning and coasted 9-6. Carlos Rosado stroked a two-run single. Juan Carlos Torres, winning pitcher Kelinton Tejada, and Andrew Bracero added RBI singles. The win moved Frankford's four-year record in Public League games (including playoffs) to 69-2.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-2005"}}

## Kevin Roche — And the 2007 Finale

Peffle's final championship came in 2007 at Campbell's Field in Camden. The hero that day was first baseman Kevin Roche — a Puerto Rico-born, South Carolina-transplanted senior who had gone hitless with a single walk as a junior the year before. Peffle, who had Roche in his math class, had asked him halfway through that junior year whether he might like to try baseball. Roche had never played organized baseball in his life.

A year later, batting cleanup against Central with Peffle's program on the line, Roche tapped out to the mound his first time up, then hit a two-run single, an RBI double to left-center that short-hopped the fence, and another RBI single on a medium-hard ball down the left-field line. Final line: 3-for-4, four RBI in a 7-5 win that produced Peffle's fifth championship in eight years.

## "I'm Going Out a Happy Camper"

Peffle's final game was the next round — a first-round PIAA Class AAAA state playoff loss to West Chester East, 14-0 in five innings, at La Salle University. As the teams came off the field, second-base umpire Jim Scott held up a brand-new baseball and asked Peffle for a pen. "This day isn't about this loss," Scott said. "It's about what we've lost." Peffle said he hadn't signed a baseball since his days as a Houston Astros farmhand.

{{PullQuote id="happy-camper"}}

He made it through the postgame talk with his players and most of a short Q&A with three reporters. Then, when he said he was going out a happy camper, the words that followed came in little more than a whisper and there was a definite moisture sighting at the inner side of his left eye.

Peffle's career ledger: 197-62 (.760) in Public League regular-season play, 38-14 in the PL playoffs, 235-76 total against Pub opposition (.756), 100-7 in his final six seasons, 153-29 across his final 11. Five Public League baseball championships. Plus a side career that included 10 PL wrestling titles as Frankford's wrestling coach and five Catholic League soccer titles as La Salle High's soccer coach — 20 seasons in that role, plus a 1985 soccer title at Plymouth-Whitemarsh.
