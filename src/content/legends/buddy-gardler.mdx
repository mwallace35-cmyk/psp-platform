## The Winningest Coach in Catholic League Basketball History

Buddy Gardler began coaching Catholic League basketball in 1969 at Bishop Kenrick and, with a two-year gap in the middle, kept at it until 2008 at Cardinal O'Hara. Thirty-nine seasons. Five hundred and sixty wins. The most by any basketball coach in the history of the Catholic League.

{{PhotoInline id="gardler-portrait"}}

Born in Ridley Park, a Bonner and St. Joseph's product himself, Gardler was a gym rat whose family orbit never left the basketball court. His summers were a rotation of swim club, hoop camps, clinics, and pickup games with his son Chris. By the time Chris was a junior at St. James High, the Gardlers had spent almost as much time playing one-on-one together as father and son anywhere in the Delaware Valley.

Then came the game that made them briefly famous.

## "I'd Do That to Any Good Player"

It was February 1986. Cardinal O'Hara needed a win over St. James to stay alive for a Catholic South playoff berth. The best player for St. James was a 5-8, 140-pound senior guard named Chris Gardler — Buddy's son. Chris had scored 12 points in the first half. In the second half, Buddy put Bill Kirschner on him and ran a diamond-and-one box the entire way. Chris scored only seven points the rest of the night. O'Hara won 71-64.

{{PhotoInline id="father-son"}}

Buddy, as the Daily News put it the next morning, "officially forfeited all chances to win any organization's Father-of-the-Year award for 1986." He also, he knew, was going home to a very quiet house.

{{PullQuote id="any-good-player"}}

{{PullQuote id="father-son"}}

The story became one of the most-told in Catholic League basketball history — equal parts tactical admiration and dinner-table comedy. It was, in miniature, the whole Buddy Gardler philosophy: find the other team's best player, and design the game around shutting him down, regardless of family tree.

## The 1992 Roman Catholic Upset

Gardler's best O'Hara team came in 1992. Roman Catholic was riding a 49-game Catholic League winning streak. O'Hara had a 6-9, 220-pound sophomore named Adonal Foyle, a transfer from Union Island in the Caribbean nation of St. Vincent and the Grenadines, who had grown up playing pickup basketball at pits that drew maybe 100 fans standing on the sidelines.

On a February night at St. Joseph's University's Alumni Memorial Fieldhouse — with close to 3,000 fans inside — Foyle shot 2-for-12 from the foul line but grabbed 16 rebounds, blocked nine shots, and altered three more as O'Hara beat Roman 53-46. The streak was over.

{{PullQuote id="foyle-crowd"}}

John Watson, a first-team All-Catholic senior that year, explained afterward that the turning point was simply the confidence the team had that season. "Last year, I had the feeling we could beat them. Then I'd see them walk in and it was, 'We're not going to beat this team.' This year, thank God, we have a big guy who can do a lot — and a whole bench filled with good players."

That same 1992 team was Gardler's first at O'Hara to reach 13-1 in the league (24-3 overall) and advance to the Catholic League semifinals.

## The 1998 CL Final

Gardler's only CL championship game appearance came in 1998, when O'Hara went 13-1 in the league and 23-4 overall. Senior forward Alex Sazonov was a first-team All-Catholic selection. The Lions reached the Catholic League final at the Palestra — the only one of Gardler's 39 seasons to get that far. They came up short in the game itself but finished with one of the best overall records in school history.

## The 39-Year Ledger
