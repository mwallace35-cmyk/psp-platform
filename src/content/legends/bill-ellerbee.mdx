## The Defense Comes First

The defining sentence of Bill Ellerbee's 20 years at Simon Gratz is the one he said casually to a reporter after his 1993 Bulldogs finished their 63-45 championship win over Franklin Learning Center to complete a 31-0 season: "And we did it without too much of a problem."

{{PhotoInline id="ellerbee-portrait"}}

By then, 8,500 people at the Civic Center had seen everything the Gratz program stood for. Ellerbee's teams that season outscored their opponents 2,078-1,173 — an average score of 69.3 to 39.1 — won games by 61, 60, 58, and 52 points, and held opponents to 18, 27 (twice), 28, and 29 (twice). Their highest defensive yield all year was 50 points. The Daily News called them the third-best team in Philadelphia scholastic basketball history.

{{PullQuote id="domination"}}

{{PullQuote id="problem"}}

Rasheed Wallace, the 6-11 center considered the top high school player in the country, finished that championship game with 16 points, 11 rebounds, and three blocks — his career total now 1,690. Sophomore point guard Terrell Stokes ran the show. Junior guard Shawn "Reds" Smith and forwards Lynard Stewart (14 points off 6-8) and Rondell Turner (6-7) completed a lineup that, by Ellerbee's own description, simply suffocated teams.

{{PullQuote id="defense-first"}}

## The First Title, 1990 — Ending a 51-Year Drought

Gratz's modern championship run began in 1990, when the Bulldogs ended a 51-year Public League championship drought dating to 1939. They beat Franklin Learning Center 80-60 at the Civic Center behind Wallace (then a 6-8 freshman) with 23 points, senior forward Harry Moore's 15 points, and senior guard Aaron McKie's 14 points and eight assists. McKie — who would play 13 years in the NBA and win a championship with the Miami Heat — was a kid from the Belfield recreation center where Ellerbee worked summers. Ellerbee had known him since he was 9 years old.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-1990"}}

A year later, Gratz repeated in a 47-43 nail-biter at the Civic Center. Wilfred Kirkaldy drew a charging foul on FLC's Faron "Meatball" Hand with eight seconds left. Levan Alston hit a one-and-one with six seconds to go. Andre Griffin and Alston led Gratz with 14 and 13 points.

## The Perfect Season and the Awarded Title

The 1993 perfect season was the peak. A year later, the story was messier. Gratz and Franklin Learning Center met in the 1994 PL final at St. Joseph's University. FLC's Michael Robinson hit a 30-foot three-pointer with one second left to win 56-55 — even though Gratz's Rasheed "Noot" Arnold had scored 34 points before fouling out. Months later, the league stripped the title from FLC for using ineligible players and awarded it to Gratz. Four championships in five seasons.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-1994"}}

## 1997 — The Marvin O'Connor Year

Three seasons after the Wallace era, Ellerbee had another championship team. His 1997 Bulldogs beat Engineering & Science 68-46 at the Palestra behind senior Marvin O'Connor (1,258 career points), Steve Kennedy (14 points, 13 rebounds, four blocks), Jarett Kearse (14 points), and Shatee "Meatball" Cooks (11 points, 10 boards). It was E&S coach C.M. Brown's only finals appearance — and his team's best scorer, junior guard Lynn Greer, had to chase Gratz's wings across the floor all night.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-1997"}}

## The Aaron McKie Year — 2001

Four years later, Ellerbee won his sixth and final championship, a grinding 40-something defensive performance against Frankford at Temple's Liacouras Center. William McNeil scored eight points in the fourth quarter, including a 60-foot drive and a 36-34 dunk that put the game away. Michael Cuffe went 4-for-4 from the line and finished with 11. Kevin "Chip" Green had 12 points, two assists, and three steals. Nicholas King blocked five shots.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="pl-2001"}}

That same year, Ellerbee spoke about Aaron McKie, by then a starting NBA guard.

{{PullQuote id="intensity"}}

McKie had given up the glamorous 40-points-a-game career a player of his talent could have had in high school. Ellerbee had told him early on: get the other kids involved, and maybe we can get a championship out of it. McKie did. They did. Twice, with McKie on the floor. And four more times after he left.

## The Retirement

In 2002, Ellerbee retired from teaching mathematics, and Philadelphia School District rules at the time forced him to relinquish the coaching job as well. His 450 wins and six Public League championships remain among the handful of best career totals in Philadelphia scholastic basketball history. His program produced three future NBA players (Rodney Shorter, Aaron McKie, Rasheed Wallace) and nine 1,000-point scorers: Brian Shorter (1,869), Rasheed Wallace (1,690), Marvin O'Connor (1,258), Aaron McKie (1,203), Shawn Smith (1,128), Percell Coles (1,090), Mark Tyndale (1,039), Rodney Shorter (~1,020), and Eddie Savage (~1,010).
