## An Instant Dynasty in Bishop Egan's First Season

When Bishop Egan joined the Catholic League in 1963, the expectation was that the Levittown Eagles would need a few seasons to figure out the competition. Dick Bedesem disagreed. In his debut year, Egan rolled through the Northern Division, beat Southern Division champion St. Joseph's Prep 36-14 at Franklin Field in front of 10,000 fans, and captured the Catholic League championship on their first try.

{{PhotoInline id="bedesem-portrait"}}

The title game pivoted on the interior running of All-Catholic halfback John Kerr (21 carries, 91 yards, TD) and fullback Paul McDowell (17 carries, 104 yards, two TDs), paired with the passing of All-Catholic quarterback Bill Creeden, who recovered from a first-period meltdown — two fumbles lost in Prep territory — to throw for a touchdown and complete two two-point conversion passes. The decisive blow was a Creeden screen pass to scatback Tim Stezzi, who cut diagonally across the field and outran Prep's Brendan Murray to the left pylon.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1963"}}

The following week at the same stadium, Egan tied Roxborough 16-16 in the 26th annual City Title game — a game in which Egan forced two late turnovers and got a game-tying safety from Paul Barczy and Joe Frazer with 1:35 remaining.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="ct-1963"}}

## The 1966-67 Back-to-Back City Title Run

By 1966, Bishop Egan was the standard in Catholic League football. The Eagles finished the regular season 7-1 in the Northern Division, routed West Catholic 39-6 in the CL final at Kennedy Stadium, and then shut out Franklin 27-0 for the City Title at Franklin Field — a championship double achieved again in 1967. With those back-to-back City Titles, Bedesem's Eagles became only the third program in city history to win consecutive City crowns, joining St. Joseph's Prep (1938-39) and Southern (1944-45).

The 1966 CL final belonged to junior Larry Marshall, who had been forced into the starting backfield at midseason due to injury and responded by scoring three touchdowns against West Catholic — a 1-yard rushing score, a receiving score, and a 77-yard kickoff return. Fullback John Lyons added 108 yards on 13 carries; star linebacker Gary Gray recovered two fumbles; Bob Migliorino and Craig Kugler each intercepted two passes; and Tom Duffy broke a 96-yard kickoff return for a touchdown after a midfield block by Jim Suravicz.

In the 1966 City Title win over Franklin, quarterback Sylvester "Pancho" Micir passed five yards to Frank Yacovino for the opening touchdown and then ran for two more as Egan scored two touchdowns in the final four minutes of the first half — the first set up by Tom Duffy's 32-yard punt return, the second by Duffy's fumble recovery.

A year later, Marshall was unstoppable again. In the 1967 CL final at Temple Stadium, he sprinted 58 yards for a touchdown 43 seconds into the second half to push Egan's lead over West Catholic to 14-0, and the Eagles rolled 27-8 behind scores from Yacovino, Mike Goetz, and Jim Ryan. In the City Title game a week later, Marshall churned for 182 yards and two scores on 20 carries, and Goetz added 101 yards on eight carries with two more TDs as Egan crushed Central 28-12.

Marshall — the man in the driver's seat for all of it — would go on to play seven seasons in the NFL as a punt and kick returner.

## The Dramatic 1969 Championship

The 1969 CL final at Temple Stadium was the strangest of Egan's five title games. The Eagles rolled up 317 yards rushing against St. James behind Dennis Molloy (20-123), Bob Lamina (18-102), and Ed McDowell (18-74), yet remained scoreless for 42 minutes as two apparent touchdowns came back on penalties. Finally, with 6:05 remaining, McDowell ran 15 yards for a touchdown and Tim Koch added the extra point that proved decisive. Brian Sweeney and Lou Siesser sealed it with late interceptions, and Egan survived 7-6 against a St. James team that had come in averaging 37 points per game.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1969"}}

The City Title game that followed, against Al Angelo's Frankford Pioneers, was a heavyweight bout. Mike Friel's 72-yard touchdown pass to Ed McDowell put Egan up 7-0 inside the first 70 seconds, but Frankford's Warren Mays threw three touchdown passes (to Gregory Taylor, Mike Biddle, and Ernie Kinzler) for a 20-7 halftime lead. Egan's comeback began when tackle Jim Helstrom recovered teammate Bob Lamina's fumble for a touchdown. Then Friel ran 33 yards for a score and McDowell broke off an 84-yard touchdown run — his 22-carry, 209-yard afternoon raising his career total to 2,258 yards. Lamina added 100 yards to finish his own career at 2,117.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="ct-1969"}}

## Father and Son, 1970

The fifth championship arrived in 1970 with a storyline straight out of a schoolyard fantasy. Bedesem's son, quarterback Dick Bedesem Jr., ran 18 times for 96 yards and a touchdown to help his father beat St. James 20-8 in the CL final at Temple Stadium. Mark Donahue ran for one score and threw to John Schailey for another. Punter Frank Scoz kept a drive alive with a run for a first down that set up Egan's final touchdown.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1970"}}

Bedesem's eight championship seasons at Bishop Egan produced a combined 40-4-2 record in CL play with 1,211 points for and only 373 against — an average score of 26.3 to 10.4. By the time he stepped away, Egan had been transformed from a Catholic League newcomer into the league's defining early program and a template for what Archbishop Wood, Archbishop Ryan, and St. Joseph's Prep would chase in the decades to follow.
