## 19 Seasons. 169 Wins. 16 Championships.

Across his first 19 seasons leading the West Catholic Burrs (1999-2018), Brian Fluck won **169 games and 16 championships** — nine Catholic League titles, six City titles, and the 2010 PIAA Class 2A state championship. He served as the school's head football coach and co-athletic director (alongside cheerleading coach Mary DeMasi), and he turned a program that had not won a major-sport title since 1979 into the most decorated team in modern Catholic League football.

The Burrs went **89-20** in Catholic League regular-season play under Fluck and **167-79** overall (through 2017). At one stretch they won 33 consecutive Catholic League regular-season and playoff games. Fluck's 2008 offense set the city scoring record with **741 points in 14 games** and became the first team in Philadelphia scholastic history to feature three 1,000-yard rushers in the same season.

## A 27-Year Drought, Ended

When West Catholic took the field at Northeast High's Charlie Martin Memorial Stadium on a close-to-biting cold Saturday in 2006, the Burrs had not won a major-sport Catholic League title since the 1979 baseball team. Their last football crown was in 1965. Their drought was the longest in the Catholic League. They had lost the Blue final in 2002, 2003, 2004, *and* 2005. They had started the 2006 season 2-4. Then they didn't lose another game.

Beating three-time defending champion Archbishop Wood 20-12, the Burrs got 55- and 44-yard touchdown runs from sophomores Raymond Maples and Curtis Drake, a 17-yard scoring pass from Drake to junior Rodney Blango, and a defensive masterclass from Isiah Edmond, Anthony Rhoades, Marc Holloway, and Chris Booker that held Wood to 14 rushing yards. Twelve underclassmen started for West.

> "We made some personnel changes after the Egan loss. Some young kids stepped up and the seniors showed great leadership. Once we started winning, I knew we'd get even better. This team had a different look. Intense and dedicated to working, but also relaxed."
> — Brian Fluck

## The 741-Point Offense

The 2008 Burrs were a city-history team. They scored **741 points in 14 games** — a Philadelphia scholastic record at 52.9 per game. They became the first team in Philly scholastic history to feature three 1,000-yard rushers in the same season: junior Rob Hollomon, junior QB Curtis Drake, and sophomore Raymond Maples. Hollomon shattered the city's single-season touchdown record (37, set by St. Joseph's Prep's Pat Kaiser in 2002), pushing the new mark to 39 in a Class AA quarterfinal romp over Dunmore.

That same season, fellow junior Quran Kent finished his career with a city-record **27 receiving touchdowns**. Drake — Penn State-bound — would become the program's defining quarterback. The 2008 Burrs steamrolled to the AA state final at Hersheypark Stadium and lost a heartbreaker to Wilmington in double overtime, 35-34.

## Hershey: The 2010 State Title

Two years after the Wilmington loss, West Catholic returned to Hersheypark Stadium and would not be denied. Behind quarterback Anthony Reid — who had transferred to West with three other former North Catholic players after that school closed — the Burrs put up **533 yards of total offense** in a 50-14 demolition of South Fayette that made them PIAA Class 2A state champions.

Reid threw for 124 yards and two touchdowns and ran for one. Sophomore David Williams (14-182, two TDs), Brandon Hollomon (14-113), and Joshua Mathis combined for 356 yards and four scores. Brandon Hollomon ended his career with 3,264 rushing yards — passing his older brother Rob's 3,159. West's 50 points were the most ever scored in a PIAA AA state final.

> "I really took that loss to heart these last 2 years. I didn't have them prepared. Allowed too much overconfidence. It turned into a dogfight that we couldn't win." — Brian Fluck, reflecting on the 2008 state-final loss before the 2010 redemption

## The Run: Eight Straight Catholic League Titles

From 2006 through 2013, West Catholic won **eight consecutive Catholic League championships** — two in the Blue Division and six in 2A. The 2010 title-game win over McDevitt was the program's 33rd straight CL regular-season/playoff victory. The 2013 team committed 17 penalties for 145 yards and *still* beat Neumann-Goretti 34-9 to extend the streak.

A one-year hiccup in 2014. Then back to the top in 2015 with a one-point thriller over Lansdale Catholic, 7-6. By the time Fluck's 19-year run was through, West Catholic had collected nine Catholic League championships under his watch.

## Six City Titles in Nine Years

The Burrs claimed City Championships in 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2015, and 2016. The 2016 final against Strawberry Mansion was the most lopsided City Title game in league history — West won 65-8, breaking the previous points record (56) and the largest-margin record (the previous best was 49). With only 16 players available for the Knights, Da'Vion Kidd-Jackson threw for 184 yards and four touchdowns and the Burrs had set new ceilings for what a City Title game could look like.
