## The Winningest Coach in Philadelphia City-Leagues History

Gamp Pellegrini spent 42 seasons on a Philadelphia high school football sideline. When he walked off Malvern's field for the last time on Thanksgiving 2008, he did so with 278 career wins — more than any coach in Philadelphia city-leagues football history — and 21 championships across three schools. No coach in the region had ever carried a longer ledger.

{{PhotoInline id="pellegrini-retire"}}

Pellegrini was a St. Thomas More graduate who played his college football at Delaware before beginning his coaching career at his alma mater in 1967. Three seasons there (9-20) gave him his apprenticeship. Eight seasons at St. Joseph's Prep (1970-77) made him a champion — his final Prep team went 10-3 and won the school's 1977 Inter-Ac title outright. Then he moved to Malvern Prep in 1978, and for the next 31 years he never left.

## Thirty-One Seasons on the Main Line

At Malvern, Pellegrini built something closer to a civic institution than a football program. His Friars went 219-86-8 in league and non-league play, winning 20 championships (16 outright) — including perfect 10-0 seasons in 1980 and 1995. He coached future Syracuse and NFL quarterback Ryan Nassib. He coached the Downs brothers — Derrick and Chris — back-to-back All-Inter-Ac running backs in the 1990s. He turned out a steady line of All-Catholic and All-City honorees year after year.

## The 2008 Finale — Down 21-0, Up 34-27

The final game of Pellegrini's career was the Thanksgiving rivalry game against St. Joseph's Prep — the school where he had once coached. Malvern was 9-0. A win would give him his third perfect season and his 20th championship at Malvern. Instead, SJ Prep jumped out to a 21-0 lead.

What followed was the kind of rally longtime Pellegrini-watchers had seen before: patient, methodical, and decisive once it started. Malvern came all the way back to win 34-27, finishing 10-0, taking the Inter-Ac title, and sending their coach into retirement on the single game that might have sat closest to his heart — a Thanksgiving win over the Prep. Ed "Huck" Palmer, covering the game, called it arguably Pellegrini's finest team.

## A Family Legacy

When Pellegrini stepped down, his son Kevin took over the Malvern program. Kevin coached the Friars from 2009 through 2013, going 37-17 and adding three more Inter-Ac championships to the family ledger.

Gamp Pellegrini died on June 9, 2017, at age 79. His 278 wins still top the Philadelphia city-leagues all-time coaches' list. The number isn't just a record. It's a measure of durability — four decades of Monday film sessions, Wednesday walkthroughs, Thursday captains' meetings, and Friday or Saturday kickoffs, compounded season after season into a career no one else has touched.
