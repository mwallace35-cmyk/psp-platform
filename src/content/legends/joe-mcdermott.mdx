## The 1982 Perfect Season

The story Joe McDermott's former players tell the most isn't about the pitching or the defense or the five Catholic League championships he won across his 31 seasons at Father Judge. It's about the MVP trophy that got broken into pieces on the bus ride home from Erny Field on a drab, gray May afternoon in 1982.

{{PhotoInline id="mcdermott-portrait"}}

Father Judge had just finished off a perfect 18-0 Catholic League season — 16-0 in the regular season with an 8.1 average run margin, then 2-0 in the playoffs — with a 6-1 championship win over Archbishop Carroll. Senior pitcher and first baseman Pat Waninger had just been named championship MVP after throwing a four-hitter with six strikeouts, his ninth consecutive win of the season. His ERA for the year: 0.80. His hits allowed: 39 in 61 innings. His record: 9-0 as a pitcher who didn't consider himself a pitcher.

{{PullQuote id="defense-behind"}}

Then came the bus ride.

{{PullQuote id="trophy-pieces"}}

By the time the Crusaders pulled into Father Judge on Solly Avenue, the only parts of the Catholic League baseball MVP trophy still together were the cup and "the man trying to bat." The other eight to 10 pieces had been passed to kids along the route. Waninger kept the batter and the cup. His teammates still tell the story 40 years later.

{{ChampionshipRecap id="cl-1982"}}

## Waninger's Emergency Pitching

The whole 1982 championship was nearly never his to pitch. Craig Canning — a talented returnee from the 1981 staff — had suffered a season-killing back injury before the regular season started. Waninger was supposed to help McDermott on the mound "in emergency situations only." Instead, he won nine straight and fashioned the best ERA in Catholic League baseball that spring.

{{PullQuote id="borrowed-pitches"}}

## Dennis Minich — The Shortstop Who Wanted Every Ball

The lone Father Judge RBI in the 1982 championship came from shortstop Dennis Minich, a former bench-warmer and cheer-leader who had spent his junior year watching and his senior year working. Minich batted .367 during the regular season. In the championship game, he went 1-for-2 with the Crusaders' only earned run — a two-out single to right in the sixth. The other five runs scored on Carroll errors.

"My basic feeling was that Coach McDermott was putting me out there so I could hang myself," Minich said afterward, laughing. "I had other ideas. I said as a freshman, 'I won't leave Father Judge without making All-Catholic and winning the championship.' I reached both goals, but it wasn't easy. I rode the bench last year and led cheers. I worked hard, especially on fielding. That would be my ticket to a starting job."

Defensively, Minich handled four chances cleanly in the championship game. A rival Catholic League coach said of him: "What's so great about Minich is that he wants every ground ball. Most kids stand there thinking, 'Hit it to somebody else.'"

## The Other Four Championships

The 1982 perfect season was the defining year of McDermott's 31 on the Judge sideline, but he also won four other Catholic League titles — 1976, 1985, 1987, and 2000 — and one City Title in 1976, the only one of his career. Across the full run, his Crusaders went 319-somewhere in Catholic League play and kept a steady pipeline of All-Catholic pitchers flowing — Mark Singer (1980), Craig Canning and Tom Nadolski (1981), Waninger and Mazurek (1982), Mike Wieczorek (1985), and so on through his 2006 retirement.

## The Pitch That Mattered

The most-told Waninger story — the trophy — actually sits next to a quieter one. When asked in 1982 what pitch had saved his season, Waninger credited every pitcher who had ever pitched for Father Judge before him: one curve from Canning, another from Nadolski, a knuckleball from Singer (quickly abandoned because the outfielders got too tired chasing the ones that didn't break), and the slider from McDermott himself. "I would have been more than happy being the left-handed reliever," Waninger said. "Instead, he came to look upon me as his ace."

That was McDermott: the kind of coach who relayed knowledge from one class to the next, who turned bench players into All-Catholics, and who kept a perfect-season trophy in the Father Judge baseball office that had been pieced back together by hand from parts tossed out a bus window in 1982.
